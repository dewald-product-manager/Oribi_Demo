A wide gap between land and plate.

03A deliberate policy

Local share of the market, raised step by step.

Under the Market Share Promotion scheme, importers, wholesalers and retailers must buy a minimum share of their fresh fruit and vegetables from Namibian growers each quarter to keep unrestricted import permits. The minimum has risen from 5% at the start to 47% today.

For a grower that can hold retail specification in volume, that is not a subsidy. It is a buyer with an obligation to find local supply.

04Southern Africa

The region is already packaging the opportunity for investors.

In October 2025 the SADC Secretariat and the FAO presented regional agri-food investment cases in Rome, deliberately framed as bankable projects for investors and development finance institutions. The four themes show where the region itself sees the gap.

US$0.6bnIrrigation
US$0.3bnMechanisation
US$0.4bnAgro-processing
US$0.8bnTrade facilitation

About US$2.1 billion in total, with 7.8 million direct beneficiaries projected across the portfolio. These are investment cases rather than committed funds, but they show an organised regional pipeline where there used to be none.

SADC Secretariat and FAO, Hand-in-Hand Investment Forum, Rome, 15 Oct 2025 (article dated 20 Oct 2025)

05Why resilience matters

The 2024 drought made the case.

An El Niño drought in 2024 affected about 68 million people across Southern Africa, according to SADC, and wiped out crops across the region. SADC declared a regional emergency and called for scaled assistance; close to 4 million children were projected to suffer from wasting.

Production that uses a fraction of the water, under cover, close to the customer, is a direct answer to that risk rather than a hedge against it.

SADC statement reported 17 Aug 2024; SADC roundtable, Victoria Falls, 28 Nov 2024 (article dated 2 Dec 2024)
