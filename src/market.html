The market

Africa imports what it can grow.

A continent with most of the world's unused farmland still spends tens of billions a year on imported food. Southern Africa is now organising capital to change that. Oribi is an early, working proof that it can be done.

03 A deliberate policy

Local share of the market, raised step by step.

Under the Market Share Promotion scheme, importers, wholesalers and retailers must buy a minimum share of fresh fruit and vegetables from Namibian growers each quarter to keep unrestricted import permits. The minimum has risen from 5% at the start to 47% today.

05 Why resilience matters

The 2024 drought made the case.

An El Niño drought in 2024 affected about 68 million people in Southern Africa, according to SADC, and wiped out crops across the region. SADC declared a regional emergency and called for scaled assistance; close to 4 million children were projected to suffer from wasting.

Production that uses far less water, and that sits close to the customer, is a direct response. SADC statement reported 17 Aug 2024; SADC roundtable, Victoria Falls, 28 Nov 2024 (article dated 2 Dec 2024)

07 The significance

Asset One is not a pilot. It is a working answer to the question every investor asks first: can it be done here?

Operating at scale

9,500 m² under cover, six independent growing systems, 35 jobs, 22 of them permanent, in a dry Namibian climate.

Retail standard

Supplying Namibian retailers since late 2023, Local GAP certified and registered with the Namibian Agronomic Board.

Market effect

We estimate fancy lettuce now costs 30 to 50% less than before we entered the market, with quality rising as the price fell.

A repeatable playbook

Water, nutrients, data, food safety and packing, proven in one of the harder growing climates. The next asset starts from here.

Oribi addresses one part of a much larger import gap. Its value to the collective is the proof: a team, a method and a supply relationship that work, ready to be repeated. See the flagship in detail.
