02 The gap

Demand for local supply was there. Supply at scale was not.

National policy already pulls toward local production: the Namibian Agronomic Board's Market Share Promotion scheme requires traders to buy a minimum share of fresh produce from Namibian growers. The minimum is currently 47%, up from 5% when the scheme began in 2005 (Namibian Agronomic Board). See the market data.

Retailers want consistency, shelf life and food safety. Early local production often could not offer them all three, every week. That is the gap we built into.

06 How we grow

Aquaponics is the method, not the headline.

Fish provide the nutrient base. We measure the water, add precisely what each crop stage needs, and recirculate it. Fish do not supply everything a crop needs, which is why most commercial projects fail. We test every system, make small regular water changes, and harvest lettuce up to 11 times a year to the same specification each time.

08 What it took

We did not get this right the first time.

Early production was inconsistent, in ways a hobby system can live with and a retail supplier cannot. We reviewed the whole process, rebuilt how we manage water and nutrients, and wrote our own production tracker.

In 2026 we doubled the aquaponic growing area, added the net house and seedling house, and expanded the packhouse.
