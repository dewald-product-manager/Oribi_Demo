The opportunity

Namibia imports food it could grow and process.

Sub-Saharan Africa imported US$62.8 billion of food in 2024. In Namibia the gaps can be named one category at a time, and each one points at a possible project.

01 The gap already closed

Fresh leaf: grown at home.

Most of Namibia's lettuce and herbs came by road from South Africa. Since late 2023 the Okahandja farm has supplied Namibian retailers from 70 km outside Windhoek.

We estimate fancy lettuce in Namibia now costs 30 to 50% less than it did before we entered the market, and quality rose as the price fell. That is what closing one gap looks like.

02 The gaps still open

Processed products, potatoes and fruit.

Each of these is bought from outside the country today. Each is a candidate for the same method: size the gap, build the case, then build and run the local supply.

03 The policy

Local share of the market, raised step by step.

Under the Market Share Promotion scheme, importers, wholesalers and retailers must buy a minimum share of their fresh fruit and vegetables from Namibian growers each quarter to keep unrestricted import permits. The minimum has risen from 5% at the start to 47% today.

For a grower that can hold retail specification in volume, that is not a subsidy. It is a buyer with an obligation to find local supply.

04 The region

The same gaps, across Southern Africa.

In October 2025 the SADC Secretariat and the FAO presented about US$2.1 billion of regional agri-food investment cases, covering irrigation, mechanisation, agro-processing and trade. They are investment cases rather than committed funds, but they show the region organising around the same gaps.

SADC Secretariat and FAO, Hand-in-Hand Investment Forum, Rome, 15 Oct 2025
