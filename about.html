About

A collective of investors and operators.

Oribi brings together people from finance, farming, energy and business structuring. We find food Namibia imports that it could produce or process at home, and we build and run the operations that replace it.

01 How the collective works

One method, four steps.

The collective is managed through the Agtech Management Trust. Each project follows the same path, and the same people stay with it from the first numbers to the first delivery.

  1. Step 1

    Identify an import gap

    Find food that Namibia imports at a cost it should not have to carry, and size the gap.

  2. Step 2

    Structure

    Build the technical and financial case, and put supply, offtake and funding in place.

  3. Step 3

    Build

    Choose the technology that does the job best, and build the operation.

  4. Step 4

    Operate

    Run it, supply Namibian customers, and carry what we learn into the next project.

The first project is the Okahandja farm, one of the largest aquaponics facilities in Africa, with 9,800 m² under cover. It began supplying Namibian retailers in late 2023. Potato processing is in development. See the projects.

03 Partners in the collective

Two partners, at the table from the start.

Bravura brings structuring, governance and investor relationships. SolarSaver brings the energy. Both are part of the management collective through their principals.

Bravura

Bravura is part of the management collective through its principals. Our chairman, Willem Bodenstein, heads Bravura Namibia, the Windhoek office of the Bravura advisory group, which supports structuring, governance and investor relationships.

SolarSaver

SolarSaver finances, builds and operates solar systems for businesses across southern Africa, so clients pay for the power rather than the equipment. It is part of the management collective through its principals, and supplies the off-grid solar system that runs the Okahandja farm, backed by a generator.
