About

A collective building Namibia's capacity to grow and process its own food.

Oribi brings together people from finance, farming, energy and business structuring, with Bravura and SolarSaver as stakeholders. We find foods Namibia imports that it could make at home, and we build and run the operations that make them.

01 What we believe

The distance shows up twice.

Namibia imports a large share of what it eats. Fresh produce crosses the border by road, often from more than a thousand kilometres away. The distance shows up in the price on the shelf, and in the days of freshness lost on the way.

What has been missing is local supply at retail scale and retail standard. That is the gap we work in. We start where the import is expensive to move and quick to spoil, because that is where local production wins first.

02 What we have done

The first proof is on the shelf.

Our first project is the Oribi farm outside Okahandja. It began supplying retailers in late 2023 and doubled its growing area in 2026. It is now one of the largest commercial aquaponics facilities in Africa, with 9,500 m² under cover.

Washed, packed lettuce that once came from South Africa is now grown in Namibia, at higher quality and at a price we estimate to be 30 to 50% lower.

05 Stakeholders and strategic partners

Two partners, at the table from the start.

Bravura brings structuring, governance and investor relationships. SolarSaver brings the energy. Both hold a stake in Oribi's projects, so both grow with every farm we build.

Stakeholder · Founding team

Bravura

Bravura is a stakeholder in Oribi's projects and part of the founding team. Our chairman, Willem Bodenstein, heads Bravura Namibia, the Windhoek office of the Bravura advisory group, which supports structuring, governance and investor relationships.

Led by Willem Bodenstein, Chairman
Stakeholder · Strategic energy partner

SolarSaver

SolarSaver finances, builds and operates solar systems for businesses across southern Africa, so clients pay for the power rather than the equipment. It supplies the off-grid solar system that runs the Oribi farm, backed by a generator, and its renewable energy strategy scales with each new project.

Led by Stuart Batchelor, Director of SolarSaver
